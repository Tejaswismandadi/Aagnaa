Step 0
Resilience Maturity Assessment
Before any framework is applied — where is the organisation today? The answer determines everything about what you do first.
The problem
The method
NovaDrive
The result
The problem
The first mistake most risk managers make is jumping straight to the methodology. Walking into a Level 1 organisation and running TTR/TTS analysis immediately will fail — the data does not exist. Walking into a Level 3 organisation and starting from scratch loses credibility on Day 1. The maturity assessment calibrates your entire approach and is the first deliverable you present to leadership.
Four maturity levels
Level 1 — Reactive
No risk register. Tier 1 visibility only and incomplete. TTR/TTS never calculated. Disruptions managed by whoever is available. Leadership learns about disruptions from production impact.
Level 2 — Aware
Risk register exists but updated irregularly. Some TTR/TTS for top-spend suppliers. Single-source suppliers known. No Tier 2 mapping. Financial monitoring informal.
Level 3 — Structured
Full TTR/TTS for P1/P2 suppliers. P1/P2/P3 tiering with formal ownership. Some Tier 2 mapping. Altman Z monitoring running. Mitigation plans exist but no black swan playbooks.
Level 4 — Embedded
Live dashboard with real-time KRIs. Full Tier 2 + targeted Tier 3 mapping. Black swan playbooks built and tabletop-tested. Near-miss register active. ROI reported to board quarterly.
The diagnostic question
If your most critical single-source supplier went offline today, how many days before your production line stops — and how long before you could restore supply from anywhere? When nobody can answer this, the case for building the system makes itself.

Step 1
Risk Governance and Risk Appetite
A risk register without governance is just a document. Define who owns this, who decides, and what the organisation is willing to accept — before collecting any data.
The problem
The method
NovaDrive
Result + resistance
The problem
Risk management initiatives fail not because the frameworks are wrong but because the organisational structure to act on them does not exist. The risk manager identifies a P1 node, recommends dual-sourcing at $2M, and the recommendation sits in an email chain for six weeks. Three structural failures drive this every time: nobody owns risk management with cross-functional authority, the risk appetite has never been stated, and escalation thresholds are undefined.
Four governance components — all must exist
1. Ownership and authority matrix: Risk owner (VP Supply Chain) · Executive sponsor (COO) · Risk analyst · Functional contributors (engineering, finance, IT, legal). One named person per role — not a committee.

2. Escalation thresholds: TTR–TTS gap above 30 days = P1 requiring mitigation plan within 30 days. Altman Z below 1.8 = immediate intervention. Active P1 disruption = 24-hour response window. Pre-agreed by leadership before the first risk is scored.

3. Risk appetite vs risk tolerance:
Risk appetite — strategic, board level
How much risk the organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives. Qualitative. Example: "We will not accept supply chain risks that could halt production for more than 14 days without a pre-approved mitigation plan."
Risk tolerance — operational, executive level
Specific measurable thresholds that operationalise the appetite. TTR–TTS gap above 30 days = P1. Altman Z below 1.8 = intervention. These numbers remove ambiguity from every mitigation investment decision.
4. Review cadence: Weekly signal monitoring · Monthly P1 review · Quarterly full register refresh · Annual audit and tabletop exercise · Out-of-cycle on trigger events

Why programs first — not suppliers: a supplier who serves an $820M program and a supplier who serves a $10M program may look identical on a supplier list. They are completely different risk priorities. Programs are the financial foundation. Every risk score is only meaningful in relation to the programs it threatens. Collecting penalty clause data is the step most risk registers skip — and it consistently understates impact by 15–25%.
