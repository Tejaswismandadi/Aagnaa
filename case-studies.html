A novel coronavirus in Wuhan became a global economic shutdown in 12 weeks. Every assumption about supply chain continuity was invalidated simultaneously. The companies that survived were not lucky - they had built buffer into the white space.

Lesson 1
Geographic diversification is insufficient for a global simultaneous disruption. Only systemic resilience - financial buffers, flexible capacity, pre-built protocols - works. Single-country diversification did not help in COVID because all countries were affected.
Lesson 2
The survival window is binary. Companies with 45+ days of P1 safety stock survived and recovered. Companies with JIT 7-day buffers halted within 2 weeks and took months to restart. The investment in buffer was trivially small compared to the loss.
Lesson 3
Pre-authorised response protocols are not bureaucracy - they are the mechanism that converts a good plan into fast action. Companies with pre-authorised air freight, pre-negotiated safety stock builds, and pre-approved alternate sourcing responded in hours. Others in weeks.
Lesson 4
The sub-tier semiconductor shortage was predictable from sub-tier mapping. Companies that had done Tier 2 disclosure in 2019 knew their exposure in January 2020. Companies without it discovered it in Q3 2020 - 6 months too late to act.

Suez Canal · March 2021
One ship. 6 days. $9 billion per day in halted global trade.
The Ever Given grounding proved that a single logistics node failure can cascade into a global supply chain event within hours. The response window was days - not weeks. Only pre-built responses worked.

Key lesson
Logistics disruption playbooks must pre-authorise the response - including the cost threshold - before the event. Every day of approval time is a day of production risk. The Suez Canal was resolved in 6 days. Companies with 7-day TTS and a 4-day approval process had zero margin.
Framework insight
Single-port dependencies are logistics single-source risk. The same TTR/TTS logic that applies to supplier single-source applies to logistics routes. Any P1 component transiting a single port with no alternate routing plan is a logistics P1 risk - regardless of how stable that route has historically been.

Japan Earthquake · March 2011
The event that proved sub-tier visibility is not optional.
The Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami destroyed facilities across Japan's manufacturing heartland. Companies with Tier 2 visibility knew their exposure within hours. Companies without it discovered it weeks later - when their production lines had already stopped.

Sub-tier insight
Toyota - widely considered the benchmark of supply chain management - took 6 months to fully recover from the 2011 earthquake because their sub-tier visibility below Tier 1 was limited. The lesson was so definitive that Toyota fundamentally changed their supplier mapping approach. AGNA incorporates that lesson as Step 6 of the methodology.
Geographic insight
The earthquake showed that geographic concentration of Tier 2 suppliers is as dangerous as Tier 1 concentration. Multiple Tier 1 suppliers, all sourcing specialty materials from the same Tōhoku region, created a hidden diamond dependency that only became visible when the earthquake hit.
